Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020

A practical guide to establishment coverage, registration, occupational safety, health, welfare, working hours, contract labour and migrant workers.

Content version: 1.0 · Reviewed: 19 November 2026 · General employer guide

Legal-status and applicability notice

The OSH Code consolidates central laws concerning occupational safety, health and working conditions for specified establishments and worker categories. Coverage varies by establishment type and statutory definitions. Identify the appropriate Government and applicable rules before using a compliance checklist. This page is educational guidance, not a legal opinion. Confirm the particular provision, commencement instrument, appropriate Government, rules, scheme and establishment facts before taking action.

Establishment coverage and definitions

  • Classify each site and activity: factory, mine, dock work, building or other construction work, plantation, motor transport, contract labour, inter-State migrant work or another covered establishment.
  • Check the Code’s definitions of establishment, factory, employer, employee, worker, contractor and inter-State migrant worker. Do not assume one headcount threshold applies to every chapter.
  • Determine the appropriate Government for the establishment and the specific matter.

Registration, licensing and common processes

  • Determine whether establishment registration, common licence, contractor licence or another statutory permission is required.
  • Confirm the authority, portal, application particulars, fee, validity, amendment and renewal process from the applicable rules and notifications.
  • Maintain registrations, licences, contractor details, renewals and compliance with licence conditions.

General employer duties and workplace safety

  • Identify workplace hazards and implement suitable preventive measures, safe systems of work, information, instruction, training and supervision.
  • Maintain plant, machinery, access routes, protective equipment and welfare facilities as required by the Code and rules.
  • Document risk assessments, safety policy, induction, toolbox talks, inspections, maintenance, corrective action and incident investigations.

Health, medical examinations and welfare

  • Identify health-surveillance and medical-examination requirements for the establishment, occupation and worker category.
  • Check welfare facilities such as drinking water, washing facilities, first aid, rest rooms, canteens, crèches and other facilities where applicable.
  • Keep examination, occupational-disease, safety-committee, training and statutory-reporting records with appropriate confidentiality controls.

Working hours, rest, overtime and leave

  • Verify daily and weekly working-hour limits, rest intervals, spread-over, overtime and leave from the Code and applicable rules.
  • Do not use a generic working-hours chart without checking the sector, establishment type, shift and State/Central rules.
  • Maintain attendance, shift rosters, overtime approvals, leave records and wage-payment proof.

Appointment letters and employee information

  • Review the statutory appointment-letter requirement and prescribed particulars for covered employees.
  • Keep signed copies, acknowledgements, service particulars and records of changes to job or establishment details.

Contract labour and principal-employer controls

  • Determine whether the contract-labour chapter applies, including the relevant establishment and contractor thresholds and exemptions.
  • Verify contractor licensing, principal-employer registration, contract scope, worker lists, wage-payment arrangements and welfare/safety responsibilities.
  • Retain licences, deployment records, attendance, wage evidence, filings and periodic review records. A contract clause alone does not remove statutory duties.

Inter-State migrant workers

  • Test the statutory definition using recruitment/migration facts, wage criteria and other conditions in force; do not rely only on the worker’s home-State address.
  • Check applicable benefits, journey allowance, registration, records and reporting requirements in the Code and applicable rules.
  • Maintain the evidence used to classify the worker and the benefits paid or arranged.

Employment of women and safeguards

  • Review statutory conditions for women working in establishments and any work/time restrictions or permission requirements.
  • For work outside the ordinary permitted time window, check the exact consent and safety safeguards under the Code and applicable rules. Consent alone should not be assumed sufficient.
  • Document transport, security, working environment and other safeguards where required.

Accidents, dangerous occurrences and occupational disease

  • Create a reporting workflow for accidents, dangerous occurrences and occupational diseases within the statutory time and form requirements.
  • Preserve incident details, witness statements, medical records, statutory notices, investigation findings and corrective-action evidence.

Employer action plan

Use these as starting controls. Convert them into a live compliance checklist only after confirming legal applicability to the establishment.

01. Site-wise legal register

Classify every establishment and activity; determine appropriate Government, coverage and applicable chapters.

02. Licences and registrations

Maintain a register of establishment registrations, common licences, contractor licences, renewals and licence conditions.

03. Safety management

Maintain risk assessments, safety policy, training, PPE, maintenance and inspection records.

04. Working-time controls

Reconcile attendance, shifts, rest intervals, overtime, leave and wage payment.

05. Contractor governance

Verify contractor licences, worker lists, wage/safety evidence and statutory responsibilities.

06. Incident response

Maintain an escalation matrix for accidents, dangerous occurrences and occupational diseases.

07. Worker-specific safeguards

Review statutory protections for women, contract labour and inter-State migrant workers where applicable.

Frequently asked questions

Does the OSH Code apply to every business in the same way?

No. Scope depends on establishment type, activity, statutory definitions, thresholds, exclusions, appropriate Government and applicable rules.

Can the principal employer rely only on a contractor’s licence?

No. Assess both parties’ statutory roles and retain evidence of required registration, licences, worker deployment, wages and welfare/safety controls.

Are working hours and leave identical across every industry?

No. Check the relevant Code provisions, Central or State rules and sector-specific requirements.

Is consent alone enough for women’s night work?

Do not assume so. Check the exact statutory and rule-based safeguards, including safety and transport requirements applicable to the establishment.
