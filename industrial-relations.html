Industrial Relations Code, 2020

A practical guide to trade unions, negotiating arrangements, standing orders, grievance redressal, strikes, industrial disputes and workforce actions.

Content version: 1.0 · Reviewed: 19 November 2026 · General employer guide

Legal-status and applicability notice

The Code consolidates the central framework on trade unions, conditions of employment, standing orders and investigation/settlement of industrial disputes. The Industrial Relations Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 modifies section 104 and is part of the current legal text. This page is educational guidance, not a legal opinion. Confirm the particular provision, commencement instrument, appropriate Government, rules, scheme and establishment facts before taking action.

Establishment, industry and worker status

  • Prepare a profile of the establishment, activity, work locations, employer and worker categories.
  • Apply the Code’s definitions of industry, industrial establishment or undertaking, employer and worker. A job title alone does not determine worker status.
  • Determine the appropriate Government. This affects the applicable rules, authority and procedural route.

Works Committee and grievance redressal

  • Check whether the statutory worker-strength test and any direction of the appropriate Government trigger a Works Committee requirement.
  • Assess the Grievance Redressal Committee requirement and, where applicable, document constitution, representation, procedure and timelines.
  • Retain appointment orders, member details, minutes, grievance registers, decisions and evidence of communication.

Trade unions and negotiating arrangements

  • Review trade-union registration, membership, constitution, records, returns and other applicable requirements.
  • Where multiple registered trade unions exist, assess the Code’s negotiating-union and negotiating-council mechanism using the statutory membership tests.
  • Keep reliable membership verification records and a documented process for recognising the negotiating representative.

Standing orders

  • Determine whether the establishment meets the Code’s standing-orders coverage threshold and check relevant notifications and rules.
  • Review the applicable model standing orders and the certification/modification procedure.
  • Check whether standing orders address classification, attendance, shifts, leave, misconduct, suspension, termination and grievance processes as required.

Strikes, lock-outs and industrial disputes

  • Before any strike or lock-out, check notice requirements, prohibited periods, pending proceedings and statutory restrictions applicable to the establishment.
  • Map internal escalation, conciliation, voluntary arbitration and tribunal processes. Maintain notices, replies, minutes, settlements and proceedings in a central case file.
  • For a live dispute, verify the specific procedural provision and timetable against the current Code and rules.

Lay-off, retrenchment and closure

  • Before a workforce action, classify the establishment and affected persons; assess worker status, service, headcount and the applicable prior-permission provisions.
  • The Code has a special prior-permission framework for specified industrial establishments. Do not assume that one workforce threshold or process applies to every establishment.
  • Review notice, reasons, compensation, service records, prior permission and other statutory conditions before issuing a decision.
  • Record compensation calculations, payments and supporting evidence.

Worker re-skilling fund

  • The Code provides for a Worker Re-skilling Fund in relation to retrenchment. Check the statutory contribution basis, timing and applicable rules for the specific case.
  • Retain the retrenchment order, wage calculation, contribution proof and evidence of payment or transfer required by the applicable framework.

2026 amendment to section 104

  • The Industrial Relations Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 received Presidential assent on 16 February 2026 and states that it is deemed to have come into force from 21 November 2025.
  • The amendment substitutes section 104(1) and adds section 104(1A). It provides for continued functioning of Tribunals and statutory authorities under the repealed enactments until the corresponding authorities under the Code become functional.
  • Read the Amendment Act with the principal Code; an unamended bare-act copy may not show the current provision.

Records and governance

  • Maintain union and committee records, standing orders, notices, settlements, disciplinary proceedings, service records and workforce-action approvals.
  • Create a prior legal-review control for strikes/lock-outs, lay-off, retrenchment, closure and changes to service conditions.

Employer action plan

Use these as starting controls. Convert them into a live compliance checklist only after confirming legal applicability to the establishment.

01. Worker/establishment mapping

Prepare a verified headcount and category matrix, including contractor and supervisory roles.

02. Union and committee governance

Check registration, negotiating status, Works Committee and grievance committee applicability.

03. Standing orders

Test coverage and maintain applicable model/certified standing orders and communication proof.

04. Dispute workflow

Use approved templates and escalation controls for notices, conciliation and settlements.

05. Workforce-action approval

Require legal review before lay-off, retrenchment, closure or major changes to service conditions.

06. Re-skilling fund

Build a separate calculation and payment control where the retrenchment provisions apply.

Frequently asked questions

Does every establishment need certified standing orders?

No. First determine whether it meets the Code’s coverage conditions and the applicable rules/notifications.

Is 300 workers a universal threshold for every workforce action?

No. Thresholds are tied to specific statutory provisions and establishment categories. Check the current section and any notification by the appropriate Government.

Can a worker be excluded because the employer calls the role “supervisor”?

No. Apply the statutory definition, actual duties and relevant wage criteria; the designation alone is not decisive.

Is the original 2020 Code text sufficient?

No. Read it with the 2026 Amendment Act, commencement notification, applicable rules and later notifications.
