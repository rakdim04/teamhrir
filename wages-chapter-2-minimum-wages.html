Sections 5–14

1. Chapter overview

Chapter II establishes the minimum-wage framework under the Code. The Code provides the statutory framework; the actual rates payable are determined through notifications of the appropriate Government. A single rate should not be assumed to apply to every State, occupation, skill category or establishment.

2. Section-wise legal explanation

Section 5 – Payment of minimum rate of wages

An employer must not pay an employee less than the minimum rate of wages notified by the appropriate Government for the employee and work concerned.

  • Identify the applicable notification and its effective date.
  • Map employees to the relevant category, skill level, area and wage period.
  • Check direct and contract labour arrangements; a contractor's wage sheet alone may not establish full compliance.

HR control: Maintain a current minimum-wage notification register mapped to each establishment and employee category.

Section 6 – Fixation of minimum wages

The appropriate Government fixes minimum rates for time work and piece work. The Code permits classification by factors including skill, geographical area and arduousness of work. Rates may be fixed by the hour, day or month. The Code also provides for a minimum time rate in the circumstances specified for piece-rate work.

HR control: Match actual duties and skill requirements with the categories in the relevant notification. A job title alone may not determine the correct classification.

Section 7 – Components of minimum wages

The minimum rate may be structured as:

  1. A basic rate plus a cost-of-living allowance;
  2. A basic rate, with or without a cost-of-living allowance, together with the cash value of authorised concessions in essential commodities; or
  3. An all-inclusive rate covering the components specified in the section.

The applicable notification determines the structure and calculation method. Do not assume that all jurisdictions use an identical basic-plus-DA structure.

Section 8 – Procedure for fixing and revising minimum wages

The appropriate Government may use the committee method or publish proposals for consideration and representations. The section specifies representation requirements for committees and procedural time limits for the notification method. The Code also provides for periodic review or revision, ordinarily at intervals not exceeding five years.

Important: The ordinary review interval is not permission to ignore a wage revision notified in the meantime. Employers must implement a valid notification from its applicable effective date.

Section 9 – Central Government power to fix floor wage

The Central Government fixes a floor wage taking into account the minimum living standards of a worker. Different floor wages may be fixed for different geographical areas. Minimum wages fixed by the appropriate Government must not be below the applicable floor wage. Where an existing minimum wage is higher than the floor wage, the Code restricts reduction merely to bring it down to the floor wage.

Floor wage
Central Government baseline under section 9.
Minimum wage
Rate fixed by the appropriate Government for the relevant category and area.

The floor wage is not a substitute for checking the current applicable minimum-wage notification.

Section 10 – Employee working less than a normal working day

Subject to the statutory conditions and exceptions, an employee whose minimum wage is fixed by the day may be entitled to the full day's wages even when the employee works for less than the normal working day. The section provides exceptions, including circumstances where the employee is unwilling to work and the employer has not failed to provide work.

Example: If an employee reports for duty but is released early because the employer has no work, the full-day wage principle may be relevant. The facts and statutory exceptions must be examined before deciding payment.

Section 11 – Wages for two or more classes of work

Where an employee performs different classes of work carrying different minimum rates, the employer must pay at least the applicable minimum rate for the time spent on each class of work.

HR control: Keep reliable task and time-allocation records where employees move between work categories.

Section 12 – Minimum time-rate wages for piece work

Where the Government has fixed a minimum time rate but not a minimum piece rate for the relevant work, the statutory time-rate protection applies in the manner set out in this section.

HR control: Check piece-rate earnings against the applicable statutory minimum protection; incentive arrangements must not defeat the minimum-wage requirement.

Section 13 – Fixing hours for a normal working day

The appropriate Government may prescribe the number of working hours constituting a normal working day, intervals, weekly rest and remuneration for the rest day. The section also addresses specified categories of work for which the ordinary provisions may be adapted or exceptions may apply.

Important: Read the applicable Rules and notifications together with the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code and relevant State requirements. Do not infer identical working-hour arrangements for every establishment from section 13 alone.

Section 14 – Wages for overtime work

Where an employee whose minimum rate is fixed by the hour, day or another prescribed wage period works beyond the normal working day, the employer must pay overtime for every excess hour or part of an hour at a rate not less than twice the normal rate of wages.

HR control: Reconcile attendance, overtime approvals, overtime calculations and payroll payment. Consider any higher rate required by another applicable law, settlement, contract or policy.

3. Rules and jurisdiction

The Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 are relevant to establishments for which the Central Government is the appropriate Government. State Rules and State wage notifications must be checked for establishments within the State Government's sphere. Central Rules should not be applied indiscriminately to every establishment.

Central Rules reference Topic to verify Compliance application
Rule 3 Calculation of minimum wage rates Verify daily, hourly and monthly conversion method and rounding against the notified Rules.
Rule 4 Variable dearness allowance Track the prescribed CPI-linked revision process where applicable.
Rule 5 Normal working day Check working-hour and interval requirements for the relevant employee category.
Rule 6 Weekly rest Check weekly rest-day and related conditions before finalising rosters.

Rule references are a navigation aid. Before relying on a rule number, rate, formula or effective date, verify the exact Gazette text and subsequent amendments. The applicable State Rules may differ.

Illustrative calculation only

If a daily rate of ₹600 applies and the relevant Rules prescribe an eight-hour divisor and 26-day monthly conversion, the illustration would be ₹75 per hour and ₹15,600 per month. This is arithmetic only—not a representation that ₹600 is the notified minimum wage in any location or occupation. Use the actual notification and prescribed conversion method.

4. Forms, registers and compliance evidence

Sections 5–14 do not each prescribe a separate form. The employer should maintain records demonstrating that the applicable rate was correctly selected and paid. Prescribed registers and forms under the applicable Rules must be checked separately.

6. Common compliance risks

Risk Preventive control
Using an outdated wage notification Maintain a notification tracker and revision alerts.
